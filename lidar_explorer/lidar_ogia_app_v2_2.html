Generate builds a real 3D surface per selected capture from the LiDAR heights for exactly the view you are looking at (available at 1:32,000 and closer), painted in the chosen style, and keeps it until you press Generate again or Clear. Changing the captures or the style regenerates automatically; moving the camera needs a press of Generate.

3D vertical exaggeration 1×

Stretches the generated 3D surfaces (single capture or the A/B comparison) around their mean elevation. Exaggeration changes the displayed mesh, and profile and point queries still use the original DEM services; scene distance and area tools can measure displayed geometry.

Off: generated surfaces float just high enough to stay above the scene ground (in a mine pit that can be tens of metres). On: the ground is lowered only under the generated area, with soft edges, so the unexaggerated surfaces sit at their source elevation; nothing else in the scene changes.

How this tool works

Compare the ground surface across 11 LiDAR captures: draw a transect for profiles and trend, drape any capture over the terrain, fade two captures, or read the elevation history at a point.

Draw a transect

  1. Find the area with the search box, or navigate the map.
  2. Press Draw transect (or N), click to place points, double-click to finish. Esc cancels.
  3. Chart 1 shows the elevation profile of every capture along the transect (one line per LiDAR date). Hover to read values; scroll to zoom.
  4. Chart 2 reduces the same transect to one representative sampled elevation per capture (mean or median of the samples) and plots it through time. Net change, yearly rate and the observed elevation change (lower, higher or within the display threshold) are shown under it.
  5. The capture chips above the charts (and the list in the Transect panel) filter every chart at once.
  6. The Change block in the panel samples B − A along the same line for any two captures.

See terrain and compare

  1. Under Terrain, choose one capture (or switch to Compare two), pick a style and press Generate: each capture becomes a 3D surface for the current view. Use vertical exaggeration to make gentle relief stand out.
  2. In Compare two, one surface stays solid and the other appears as a ghost as you move the slider, so changes between the dates emerge out of the reference.
  3. Terrain tiles load as you zoom in. The pill at the bottom of the map tells you when tiles are loading, or when you are zoomed out beyond the scales the capture is published for.

Find where to look

  1. Under Heat map, choose the current view or draw an area, pick a metric and press Generate.
  2. Every ticked capture is resampled onto the same grid and compared cell by cell. Red = ground lower over time, blue = higher; small changes are hidden.
  3. The list of places to explore jumps you to the strongest lowering and rising spots and reads their point history, ready for a transect.

Slope

  1. Terrain → Style → Slope paints the generated surface by steepness in % rise (1 % = 1 m per 100 m), computed from the sampled heights in view. Zoom in for finer cells.
  2. Heat map → Change in slope shows where the ground has tilted between the earliest and latest capture: uneven lowering changes the slope, uniform lowering does not. Read it against the noise floor reported with the result.
  3. In the Transect panel, switch the change chart to Slope along the line to compare the slope of A and B and the change in end-to-end grade.
  4. Inspect lists the slope at the point for every capture, with the direction the ground falls towards.

Read a point

  1. Under Inspect press Read a point (or I) and click the map.
  2. You get the elevation from every capture at that spot with the change between captures. Copy it as CSV.

About the data

All captures are 1 m LiDAR digital elevation models hosted by OGIA. Elevation values are in metres. Differences between captures include survey and processing uncertainty; treat small changes with care and check the metadata before drawing conclusions.

Interpretation

These are observed elevation differences. Survey uncertainty, processing, datum differences and real surface changes can all contribute. A display threshold does not establish detectable subsidence.

Source grids are described as nominal 1 m DEMs. Sample spacing, actual DEM resolution and vertical accuracy are separate. Verify vertical datum and survey accuracy in the source metadata before formal use.

Capture windows follow the supplied app configuration and require owner confirmation; time calculations use their midpoints. The existing QA/QC layer describes the 2025 capture as July–August, while this catalogue uses July. Generated terrain can be lifted or exaggerated for display.
